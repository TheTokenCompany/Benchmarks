2 · What survives compression, by content category

Word keep-rate at aggressiveness 0.5. Old models drop what matters most: percent tokens keep at 17-28%, scale words ("million", "billion") at 20-25% — figures survive stripped of their magnitude. v7.1 fixed scale words (95%) but still halves money and percent tokens; safenum pins all numeric classes at ~100%. Stopwords are correctly near zero everywhere.

3 · Table rows starve while boilerplate feasts

Mean fraction of each line's words surviving, aggressiveness 0.5. Every model ranks boilerplate first. 5 · What force-keeping numbers rescues — and what it can't

plain v7.1 + force-keep numbers

Compression-induced errors at aggressiveness 0.5 by question type. Metric lookups collapse (30→4 on gpt-5-mini, 34→10 on Gemini); qualitative "novel" questions barely move (18→11, 15→6). 6 · The keep-rule race (oracle, aggressiveness 0.5, Gemini)

All variants derive from the same v7.1 keep-probs (one 4-second B200 pass); whiskers are 95% binomial CIs. Structural rules (row labels + periods, "safetab") match the ±2-word window ("safectx2") at 9 pp less token budget. "weld" (atomic value-spans, no forcing) proves span atomicity is not the failure mode — it equals plain. FUSE = OR-union with the query-aware focus model: +7.3 pp over plain for 2 pp of tokens; on top of safetab it adds up to +3.3 pp (best oracle compressed result: FUSE-safetab @ 0.3 = 73.3%). The rule-family differences sit inside each other's CIs; the rules-vs-plain gap (~25-30 pp) does not.

7 · Full documents: compression beats the uncompressed baseline

v7.1 plain v7.1 + safetab FUSE (v7.1 OR focus) + safetab full-filing control (no compression)

Same 150 questions, but the context is the entire filing (median ~104k o200k tokens; competitor "compresr" benchmarks at ~106k avg on a 128-question subset) instead of oracle pages. Long context costs the baseline 4 pp (82.7% oracle → 78.7% full-doc); safetab compression at 57% retention wins it back — 82.7%, +4 pp over the uncompressed baseline at ~43% cost savings. compresr's published table (GPT-5.2 + latte_v2): 73% → 77% at ~2×. The standalone query-aware focus model extracts ~600 tokens (0.5%) from these filings and still answers 16.7% — off this chart's x-scale but the reason the FUSE union works. FUSE + safetab extends the frontier at the aggressive end: 80.0% at 47% kept (2.1×), +4 pp over safetab alone and still above the uncompressed baseline; at lighter settings fusion is within noise. Note the compressed-vs-baseline gaps here are within single-run 95% CIs — treat the "+4 pp" as a repeated pattern (it appears at two operating points and in compresr's own table), not a single proven delta.

8 · Rehabilitating the query-aware model: 7 serving-pipeline experiments

focus, fixed pipeline (line-split + rank-budget) v7.1 plain (reference) control (no compression)

The stock focus runner scored 16.7% because its serving pipeline was broken, not the model: the punctuation sentence-splitter merges whole financial tables into one giant unit and mean-pooling dilutes the answer-row spike, while tau-thresholding has no usable operating point (scores collapse near 0). Fixes tested in one 10-second B200 pass: line-level units + rank-based token budgets. Gold-figure recall (ceiling: only 46% of numeric golds appear verbatim in any context): stock 35% of ceiling at 3.6% kept → fixed 78% of ceiling at 10% kept, 93% at 30%. Null results: pooling operator (max vs mean), word-level granularity, and stripping directive query phrasing all changed nothing (±2 pp). Accuracy: at 30% retention the fixed query-aware pipeline scores 53.3% — the best sub-40%-retention result in the study (v7.1 plain needs 47% of tokens for 48.0%) — and fuse2 (v7.1+safetab OR top focus lines) reaches 72.7% at 64% kept with figure recall at ceiling. At 10% retention accuracy collapses to ~20% despite 78%-of-ceiling recall: surviving figures lose their row/period labels, and the answerer won't commit to unlabeled numbers — the same number+label failure mode as section 5, now confirmed from a second direction. The fuse2 grid sweep (v7.1 aggressiveness × focus-line budget) set the study's best compressed result: 76.7% at 66% kept with a tighter v7.1 threshold and a bigger focus budget — tokens chosen by the query-conditioned model outrank tokens chosen by general importance. Pushing the focus budget further traces the frontier to the doorstep of control: f50 78.0% @ 72%, f70 81.3% @ 79% (1.4 pp from the uncompressed 82.7%), f90 79.3% @ 86% (flat — the last 15% of tokens buys nothing).

9 · Ceiling probes: frontier-model oracle compression & a second reader (E28-E30)

E28 — reader swap. The four champion full-filing configs re-answered by gpt-5-mini (third panel in section 0). gpt-5-mini's own full-context control is 85.3%; dscale-fin safetab@0.3 scores 86.0% at 71% kept — compression beats full context under a second, stronger reader too, and the ranking of configs is preserved (safetab@0.5 84.7% > saferow@0.5 83.3% > ws72fin 81.3%). The compression signal is reader-independent; only the absolute level shifts (+2-6 pp over Gemini).

E29 — Fable 5 as an oracle extractive compressor on the 17 never-answered questions. Claude Fable 5 (max effort) read each full filing with the question — not the gold — and emitted a strictly verbatim extraction (validated: 100% of output lines are exact substrings of the source, on all 17). Extracts were tiny (0.2-11% retention, median ≈0.6%) and, spot-checked, complete — e.g. for the 3M quick-ratio question the extract holds current assets, inventories, and current liabilities tightly juxtaposed; quick ratio = (15,754−5,280)/10,936 = 0.96 = the gold. Gemini answered from these ideal contexts: 0/17 flipped (gold-ambiguous 0/10, needs-computation 0/5, judge-artifact 0/2). Even a frontier-model oracle compressor rescues none of them — Gemini declines to compute (answered "the quick ratio is not mentioned" while staring at all three components), the 10 defective golds stay defective (Q135's gold asserts a spin-off that closed in Nov 2020), and the judge stays strict (Q133: Gemini's 10%×$700M=$70M vs the gold author's $700M/9=$77.78M). This closes the loop on the failure forensics: the 17 are reader/gold/judge failures, not compression failures — context selection on FinanceBench is effectively solved at these questions, and the reliable-flip channel through better compression is exhausted. (Caveat: union across ~60 configs did reach 133/150, so stochastic flips exist — but best-of-N against the judge raises union accuracy, not the expected single-run accuracy a config is scored on.)

E30 — Fable 5 as an abstractive compressor: 3/17 flipped. Same 17 questions, but Fable could reorganize — normalize table rows into labeled lines ("Purchases of property and equipment (capital expenditures): FY2022 = 442"), spell out units/periods, juxtapose components — while forbidden from computing or answering (leak-checked: the flipped abstracts contain only labeled raw figures, no ratios, no conclusions). Result: Q14, Q23, Q141 flip CORRECT at 0.6-14% retention (needs-computation 1/5, gold-ambiguous 2/10, judge-artifact 0/2). The extractive-vs-abstractive delta (0/17 vs 3/17) is pure representation: the same reader that answered "the quick ratio is not mentioned" while holding all three raw components will happily compute once each figure carries its statement + period + units label. Caveat: with judge noise at 4-7 flips per 150-question run, 3/17 is suggestive rather than proven — but it points at a deployable lever — if labels are correct (see E31).

E31 — heuristic labeled-render: NEGATIVE. The cheap mechanization (banner lines ### <nearest title above> | <units> | <period> prepended to kept table regions, all verbatim doc lines) was applied to both champions and run on the full 150: v7.1-safetab@0.5 82.7→80.7% (gained 3 / lost 6), dscale-safetab@0.3 84.0→78.7% (gained 3 / lost 11), zero of the hard-17 touched. Misattached banners (nearest-above title is often the wrong statement for a fragment surviving mid-document) actively mislead — worse the more table fragments survive, hence dscale's larger drop. Verdict pair: E30 proves correct per-figure labeling unlocks reader arithmetic; E31 proves approximately-correct labeling is worse than none. The representation lever needs real table structure (row label + column→period mapping) — a structured-render layer or a model that learns it — not proximity heuristics. Champions remain the unrendered safetab configs.

10 · Overnight-2: wave-2 SFT and the first RL wins (E32-E33)

E32 — wave-2 SFT (wide-halo masks). Three runs (w2-140, w2-140-s2, w2-ettin-400m), all healthy, per-question-type keep ratios finally tracking targets. Transfer verdict: w2-ettin (400M backbone) is the wave's best — budget33+safetab 78.7% @ 51%, budget22+safetab 75.3% @ 42.5%, raw budget22 60.7% @ 22% — but nothing beat wave-1's deep record (r1-ep4 68.0% @ 22%) and the v7.1-rules champions held. The early-epoch-transfers-better inversion replicated a third time (s2ep4 62.0% vs full-trained s2 57.3% at budget22).

E33 — v9 RL (reward-weighted regression): the deep Pareto moved. Trainer: taaha's RWR mechanics (K=6 sampled word-masks per item, softmax-weighted imitation, KL-to-frozen-ref, keep floor) with an end-to-end reward: render each candidate, ask gemini-3.1-flash-lite the question against it, score by deterministic numeric match to the gold (no LLM judge), minus λ·token-retention — evaluated at the 12-33% band where reward has real dynamic range (probe: survival 0.83 full → 0.17 @ 22% → 0.00 @ 12%). Found and fixed a NaN-Gumbel bug inherited from the source trainer (its exploration had been silently absent). First runs collapsed after epoch 1 (classic), but the half-epoch peak checkpoint from the s2ep4 init scored 0.75 answer-survival @ 22% (init baseline 0.667), and on the full 150: 73.3% @ 22% kept (4.5×) — +5.3 pp over the all-time deep record, +11.3 over its own SFT init — and 76.0% @ 33% (3×, +6.0 over the record). KL 0.3 then stabilized training (0.67-0.705 held across 2 full epochs, no collapse). Reward substrates regenerated from Alan's pipelines for the next rounds: LEDGAR 289 MCQs (48% yield, $6.88), SECQUE 58 (10% yield, $70.75). In flight: KL 0.15 middle-ground, iterated RWR re-anchored at the peak, and an ettin-backbone RL prep.

How the night ended (all follow-on rounds resolved). The RL gain came in exactly one step: SFT ep4 (62.0% @ 22%) → one RWR round at KL 0.3 → 74.0% @ 22%. Every attempt to extract a second step failed in an instructive way: re-anchored rounds on the same 2,000 reward items raised val (0.705→0.76→0.765) but LOWERED benchmark transfer (71.3% @ 22%) — the policy exploits a fixed reward set; a disjoint fresh-data round from the champion was flat (val 0.71) — the 140M lineage is saturated; and the ettin-400M run, despite the night's best and most stable val (0.785, +15 over its anchor), collapsed to 60.0% @ 22% on the benchmark — its gains lived in the single-centered-chunk training distribution and died under full-document sliding-window serving. Four separate instances tonight of offline metrics mis-ranking real transfer (AUPRC ep9-vs-ep4, val-vs-FinanceBench for iter2, for ettin, and the wave-1 inversion) — the eval fleet on real filings is the only selection signal we trust. Method lessons banked: select SFT checkpoints by a 200-item answer-survival read (~free), one RL round per data batch, KL 0.3 trust region, and a NaN-Gumbel bug found in the inherited RWR trainer (its exploration had been silently dead — reported to taaha).

Training recommendations

  1. Ship number protection now, before any retraining. Auto-wrapping numerics in <ttc_safe> at the API layer replicates safenum exactly: +22-27 pp on both answerers at equal token budget. This is an inference-time rule, not a model change.
  2. Train keep-bias for number + label units, not bare numbers. The dominant residual failure (6/10) is a surviving figure orphaned from its row header, segment, or period. Datagen should label the number and its identifying label span as a joint keep-unit (table row header + cell, "Q2 FY24" + figure).
  3. Add financial tables to training data with structure-preserving targets. Every pre-v7.1 model anti-selects table rows (2-3× worse than prose). Train on 10-K/10-Q statement pages where the target keeps whole table rows; FinanceBench-style QA pairs make good supervision for which rows matter.
  4. Add negative labels for boilerplate. Page furniture, ALL-CAPS headings, "accompanying notes" legalese is the best-surviving content in all configs — budget that should fund percent tokens and table rows. This is free accuracy at fixed retention.
  5. Protect percent tokens explicitly. Worst-kept numeric class in every model (6-28% in old models, 15% even in plain v7.1 @ 0.7); margin/growth questions die here.
  6. Keep scale words welded to their numbers. v7.1 already keeps million/billion at 90-96% (vs ~20% for the old family) — make this a spec'd invariant, since a bare "1,577" without magnitude is noise.
  7. Distill the query-aware keep-signal into the next model. OR-fusing the focus model's picks into v7.1 adds +7-11 pp at aggressive settings for ~2 pp of tokens, and the focus model alone answers 16.7% of questions from 0.5% of a full filing. The signal is real and cheap (a second 140M encoder pass) — ship as an ensemble now, distill into one model later.
  8. Rank models at matched retention, not matched aggressiveness. The knob maps differently per model (0.3 means 87% kept for diverse+code, 43% for bear-4.1); every cross-model conclusion here required the retention axis. Trading data looks unnecessary: the failure is table/number selection on filings, not market-data knowledge.
